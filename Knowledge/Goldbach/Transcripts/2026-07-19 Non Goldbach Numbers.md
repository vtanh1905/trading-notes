# Summary
## Non-Goldbach Numbers Overview

This lecture introduces non-Goldbach numbers and their role in the trifecta framework. They fill 12% gaps between Goldbach numbers by splitting each gap into two 6% blocks.

## Goldbach Numbers Recap

Goldbach numbers appear in pairs summing to 100, forming six pairs. For example, 3+97, 41+59, and 29+71. The high and low of a PO3 dealing range also form a pair, i.e., 0 and 100. Most ranges are 6% apart except the discount (3 to 11) and premium (97 to 89) sides, which are 8% apart.

## 12% Jumps in Goldbach Numbers

Larger jumps of 12% occur, e.g., from 17 to 29 and 29 to 41 on the discount side, and from 83 to 71 to 59 on the premium side.

## Derivation of Non-Goldbach Numbers

To fill 12% gaps, each is split into two 6% blocks. Adding 6% to Goldbach 17 gives non-Goldbach 23; another 6% lands on Goldbach 29; another step gives 35. Thus, discount non-Goldbach numbers are 23 and 35, with premium counterparts 65 and 77.

## Why "Non-Goldbach"

Goldbach's conjecture requires two primes summing to an even number. Here, 35 and 65 are divisible by 5; 77 factors into 7×11, all non-prime. 23 is prime but pairs with non-prime 77, breaking the conjecture. The others are semi-primes (product of two primes).

## Role in the Flow Layer

These non-Goldbach numbers sit around sweet spots (29 on discount, 71 on premium). Subtracting or adding 6% from these sweet spots generates levels defining the flow layer.

## Conclusion

Non-Goldbach numbers are crucial for the trifecta flow layer, filling gaps and creating necessary levels.
