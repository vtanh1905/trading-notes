# Summary

## Last Line of Defense (LLOD)

Hi, this is the final video in the trifecta layers series. We've covered the three main layers, liquidity, flow, and rebalance, along with the Goldbach inversion point.

One crucial level remains, the last line of defense, LLOD. The LLOD sits right in the middle between the rejection level and the liquidity level.

As a quick refresher, the rejection level is formed by the 3 and 97 pair, while the liquidity level is defined by the 11 and 89 partition. Its purpose is to warn you that price may be preparing a stop run above or below the current range.

When price breaches this level, it's a clear signal that the current range could be violated, either through an external range stop run or by shifting to the adjacent range as the new normal.

In the following screenshot, you can see price breaching the LLOD, finding support there, and then breaking outside the range to target the flow layer of the range above.

This level also serves as an important marker when you spot a gold bock inversion. When price moves away from a PO3 dealing range low or high, breaches the gold bock inversion point with speed,  
creating a visible gap, you should not see price trade back through the LLOD. In this example, price moved away from the high of the range, created a gap at the 83 level,  
or 17 on the discount side and headed toward the flow layer. A retrace is expected often back to the GIP, but deeper retracements can occur.

In this case, the deeper retrace was stopped precisely at the LLOD, which protected further upside. Always factor in the LLOD when managing risk on trades based on the gold Bock inversion.  
Your stop should be placed at or just below the LLOD. This level becomes especially important once you learn the Einstein trade plan. In the next example,  
Price first decided which range to use. It reentered the current range and completed the gold back inversion, signaling higher prices. After moving toward the flow layer,  
It retraced deeply, but again, the LLOD acted as strong support and halted the pullback. As you can see, the LLOD, liquidity level, and GoldBock inversion level  
work together as a group. I recommend keeping the LLOD on your chart, whenever you analyze the liquidity layer. It will help you identify the correct setups and manage risk effectively.

This concludes the entire trifecta system. I hope you'll start exploring these levels and discover how they integrate into the trade plans we'll cover in the mastery module. Have fun exploring.  
And I'll talk to you soon.
