# Summary
## Shifted Month Fundamentals

The shifted month, also known as the look-back partition, redefines the start of each trading month. Instead of beginning on the 1st, the start date is calculated using a sequence based on the number 9. Start with 9 and add 9 repeatedly until reaching 126, yielding 14 numbers (all divisible by 9). Since a year has only 12 months, two numbers are excluded: 09 (no month 0) and 90 (no day 90). The remaining 12 numbers correspond to the start dates of each shifted month.

To convert a number into a month and day:

- For two-digit numbers: the first digit is the month, the second is the day.
    
- For three-digit numbers: the first two digits are the month, the last digit is the day.
    

Example: 117 splits into 11 (November) and 7, giving November 7th.

If the calculated date falls on a weekend, shift forward to the next business day.

## Key Numbers and Important Days

Each shifted month has an active number: the two-digit number representing the start date (e.g., March 6th uses 36, July 2nd uses 72).

Three special days (Goldbach days) are significant: 3, 11, and 17.

- On day 3: Look for gaps, blocks, or liquidity runs whose size aligns with the active monthly number (e.g., for March 6th, look for 36-point moves). Analyze on higher PO3 time frames (81-minute or 243-minute). Expect price to react and push toward the opposite side of the structure.
    
- On days 11 and 17: Anticipate a liquidity run in the opposite direction, either retracing into prior range or delivering a full reversal.
    

## Application

When a new month begins, count days from day 1 (opening day). Use the active number to identify potential turning points. Practice on charts to recognize the pattern.
