# Summary
## Introduction to the Einstein Trade Plan

Hello friends of Hopi. Today, we're diving into another exciting episode on the Goldbach Trifecta framework. This time, we're exploring trade plans. This is the first trade plan I ever shared based on Goldbach levels. And it remains one of my absolute favorites to trade.

## Origin of the Name

You might be wondering, how did Hopey come up with the name Einstein Trade Plan? There's a fascinating reason behind it. And it all connects to one specific PO3 number, 27. When you study Einstein's mass energy equivalence and his famous formula, E equals MC squared, something interesting emerges. As many of you know, I was once a student of the inner circle trader, where I tried solving one of his puzzles. I was particularly curious why he used three different terms to describe the middle of something: equilibrium, mean threshold, and consequent encroachment. If you take the first letter of each word, you get E M T C E, or E equals M times C exponentiation. That looks strikingly similar to E equals MC squared, doesn't it? Now here's where it gets even better. There's one specific number, 299792458. If we group it into three sets of three digits, we get 299, 792, 458. Divide each by our beloved PO3 number 27 and round the results: 299 divided by 27 equals 11, 792 divided by 27 equals 29, 458 divided by 27 equals 17. Don't these form a beautiful set of Goldbach numbers?

## Building the Trade Plan

With this elegant set in hand, we can now build a complete trade plan around them. Let's dive in. The system is actually very straightforward. You start with a defined range, either a standard PO3 dealing range or a fractal range, a Goldbach fib sandwiched between two Goldbach levels. You then observe how price oscillates around the range high or low, before it suddenly expands, leaving a gap between the 11 and 17 levels for longs, or the 89 and 83 levels for shorts. Price then moves toward the flow layer. Here, stay flexible. Under normal conditions it heads for the 29 or 71 level, but it doesn't always reach it. Instead of insisting on the exact 29 level, simply assume price is heading into the discount flow layer for longs, or the premium flow layer for shorts. You may see it stall at the edge of that layer. Next, watch for a retrace. Ideally, it pulls back to the 17 long or 83 short level, but it can go deeper toward the 11 or 89 levels. This deeper retrace is your entry zone. Place your protected stop below the swing low of the range for longs or above the swing high of the range for shorts. Your target is at least the rebalance zone. Again, be flexible. Don't demand that the 50 equilibrium level is always tagged. Instead, monitor whether the rebalance zone holds. This is a critical make or break level. Price may retrace deeply, fully reverse, or break through the rebalance layer and continue to the final objective, the opposite side of the range. That's when the Einstein trade plan is fully completed.

## Conclusion

As you can see, this trade plan has just a few moving parts. All you need are three Goldbach numbers, either the discount or premium variant. A basic understanding of blocks and gaps, and you're ready to go. I hope you explore the plan. It's straightforward and has the potential to deliver excellent results.
