# Summary

## Calibrated Price Levels

Calibrated price levels work hand in hand with Goldbach levels. It is also essential to keep an eye on the mid levels that sit between any two Goldbach levels.

When you examine a chart closely, price often falls slightly short of a Goldbach level. This does not mean the Goldbach level is invalid. If you look to the left on the chart, you'll typically see that this level was already touched and respected in the past. Each time price touches and rejects that level, it forms a swing point, usually visible as a wick on the candle. That wick is key.

To create a calibrated level, simply extend a horizontal ray from the extreme of that wick forward in time. This calibrated level will often act as a precise magnet or barrier, attracting price back toward it or repelling it to drive movement toward the opposite side.

## Examples

On a 27-minute chart, price failed to touch the Goldbach level. Looking to the left, price created a swing at this level. Zooming in to a lower power of three time frame may be needed to see the swing that forms at the Goldbach level. Using the calibrated level, price rejects it and drives higher.

It can happen that price touches the calibrated level and creates liquidity. This liquidity is later ran right into the Goldbach level, to drive price again to the opposite side. This does not always happen, but it's good to anticipate it can occur occasionally.

## Conclusion

Always keep an eye on price swings that occur at the key Goldbach levels. To identify these levels, look to the left on the chart.
