# Summary

## Goldbach Inversion Point (GIP) Overview

The Goldbach Inversion Point (GIP) helps determine future direction. It sits in the liquidity layer and serves as a key reference to gauge whether price will perform a stop run and return to the previous range or shift into an entirely new range.

## GIP Levels and Price Behavior

Price oscillates around the low of the current range, which is also the high of the adjacent range below. The GIP is positioned at the 17 level (discount) and 83 level (premium). When price finds support and arrives from the range above, it may set up an external range stop run.

## Trading with the GIP

- If price breaks lower through the GIP, it signals rejection of the upper range and confirms continued use of the current lower range.
    
- If price moves back above the low of the range and breaches the GIP upward, the lower range serves as a stop run range, and price returns to the previous range for continuation higher.
    
- When price moves above the GIP, it acts as a market structure shift (change in state of delivery).
    

## Confirmation and Action

For confirmation, price should make a fast, decisive move away from the range, typically starting from the 11 level (when heading higher) or the 89 level (when heading lower). Price should breach the 17 or 83 level and leave a clear gap (e.g., hippo gap). This confirms the Goldbach inversion has taken place, allowing traders to assume price will continue toward the next layer (flow layer).

## Final Line of Defense

When price moves away from the 11 or 89 level and completes the inversion, the final line of defense should protect price.

## Importance

Having the GIP on charts is like having an automated market structure shift indicator. It clearly tells whether a range will hold or break and when to look for high probability setups like the Einstein plan. Without it, you're essentially trading blind.
