# Summary
## The Rebalance Layer Overview

The rebalance layer is described as the toughest layer to trade, often called the 'chop zone' due to choppy, directionless price action (consolidation). It is a temporary pause in price action where price decides its next move—either breaking through to the other side of the dealing range or failing and dropping back to the origin high or low.

## Key Levels in the Rebalance Layer

The rebalance layer consists of three key levels:

- **Internal liquidity Goldbach levels**: 41 (discount) and 59 (premium).
    
- **Internal rejection Goldbach levels**: 47 (discount) and 53 (premium).
    
- **The 50 level (equilibrium)**: Separates the discount block from the premium block.
    

## Make-or-Break Behavior

Price tends to pause at the 50 level, oscillating around the middle of the range in consolidation. This is the make-or-break moment: price either reverses here and breaks below (acting as resistance) or breaks above and continues toward the next flow layer. In the break scenario, the rebalance layer acts as support/resistance, causing price to retrace and then head toward the range low or high.

## The Account Killer Profile

A third profile exists: the 'account killer' where price oscillates repeatedly without committing, running stops in classic power of three behavior. Traders are warned to step aside if they lose twice in a row.

## Summary

The rebalance layer is a critical juncture within a dealing range, working with the liquidity and flow layers to define price action.
